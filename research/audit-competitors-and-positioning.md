# Audit Workbench: competitors and positioning hypotheses

Research date: 2026-11-06. Audience ambition: all auditors; first segment and workflow remain narrower decisions. Companion documents: [form and interviews](audit-user-discovery.md), [Inflo/Fieldguide evidence](audit-competitor-supplement.md).

## Evidence limits

This is desk research, not hands-on product benchmarking. Official pages establish advertised or documented capabilities. Customer reviews report experiences, sometimes older, incentivized or drawn from a different audit discipline. Review-platform summaries are not independent tests. Ratings are snapshots, not comparable measures of audit quality. Do not generalize a few complaints into a market-wide verdict.

The old repo's broad enterprise-price range is not a verified basis for claiming small firms are excluded. Current comparable quotes, seat minima and package contents were not established. Likewise, absence from a webpage does not establish a missing capability.

Excel plus established firm templates is also a critical comparison: interviewees may prefer a familiar, adequate workflow even when they dislike parts of it. This is a discovery hypothesis, not a quantified market-share claim.

## Recommended differentiation hypotheses

1. **Less preparation before useful work.** Validate input completeness, explain missing columns and reconciliation breaks, and preserve reusable mappings. Test total effort including conversion and corrections. Fixed templates are a prototype simplification; they may undermine the claimed time savings.
2. **A reviewer can reproduce every result.** Tie a difference or flag to source rows, rule, threshold and calculation; distinguish data-quality problems from items needing audit investigation. Measure whether another auditor can verify the work without rebuilding it. Competitors offer traceability too, so superiority needs a task comparison.
3. **An add-on that fits the engagement file.** Prioritize useful Excel outputs and existing handoffs if interviews support them. Measure extra uploads and duplicate documentation; a separate app can add friction.
4. **One complete, worthwhile workflow.** Revenue/AR is the repo's candidate, not the validated winner. Finish an identifiable task from permitted input to accepted workpaper before expanding to other cycles or audit disciplines.
5. **Practical adoption for the first segment.** Test setup time, training, buyer approval, processing location and commercial expectations. Predictable pricing and synthetic demos may help, but neither establishes willingness to pay or production readiness.

Proposed positioning to test: “Turn Revenue/AR data into traceable reconciliations and reviewable Excel workpapers, with less preparation and rework.” This is a proposed outcome, not a shipped-product claim.

Do not lead with “AI auditor,” “detects fraud,” “compliant automatically,” or “the only affordable option.” The researched evidence does not establish those claims. Browser-side processing and synthetic-only AI are current design choices, not evidence that a firm's adoption requirements have been met.

## What would change the recommendation?

- If preparation dominates, import flexibility may matter more than extra analytics.
- If existing tools already reconcile efficiently, investigate reviewer handoffs or another repeated pain instead.
- If internal auditors prioritize evidence collection and remediation, treat that as a distinct expansion hypothesis rather than relabeling Revenue/AR.
- If no permitted pilot or approval route exists, synthetic usability testing can continue, but commercial validation remains incomplete.
- Require a concrete before/after task comparison before claiming faster work, fewer unnecessary flags or better reviewability.
