# Company-accountant alternative: competitive evidence

Researched 2026-11-06. Scope: FloQast, BlackLine, Numeric. Vendor descriptions establish advertised capabilities, not independently measured outcomes. G2 observations below are individual experiences, not representative estimates or proof that a reported limitation remains unresolved. No pricing comparison was established.

Access limitation: Numeric's audit-evidence help article was available as indexed search text during this research; the parent researcher encountered a login redirect when opening it directly. Its description is documentary evidence, not a tested export.

## Decision implication

**Inference:** Switching from external auditors to company accountants changes the job and buyer, but does not escape established competition. Reconciliation, exception flags, flux explanations, supporting documentation, and audit preparation are already explicit close-software capabilities. An initial company-accountant proposition should be a narrowly specified Revenue/AR close task, not a generic “AI financial close” platform.

The current prototype's Revenue/AR reconciliation, flags, Excel workpapers, and synthetic data fit a demonstration of that task. Synthetic scenarios can demonstrate explainability and catch known errors; they do not establish reliability on customer exports or willingness to buy. This assessment uses the prototype description supplied for this research, not a fresh code audit.

## Differentiating hypotheses to test, not established market gaps

1. **One export-to-workpaper Revenue/AR workflow:** A controller with a specific ERP/billing combination may value a quick upload that produces an explainable reconciliation and reviewer-ready workbook without configuring a full close platform. Measure time to first useful result and compare against the team's actual Excel procedure. Competitor setup complaints motivate the test; they do not prove demand for a separate product.
2. **Specific exception investigation:** Preserve invoice/customer/contract/product IDs, show source rows and deterministic reasons behind every flag, and track resolution evidence. Numeric's revenue-granularity review suggests an interview question; it is not proof Numeric currently lacks those fields or that others do.
3. **Portable evidence handoff:** Test whether an accountant and their external auditor can both use the generated analytical workbook with fewer follow-ups. Ask what they cannot currently export cleanly. Numeric already exports close activity evidence, so distinguish analytical traceability and the particular workbook artifact precisely.
4. **A supplement to existing tools:** Investigate whether teams still maintain a specific Revenue/AR spreadsheet outside their close system. Replacement of an entire close platform is a much larger hypothesis than completing that remaining task.

## Buyer choice and next interviews

**Inference:** The company-accountant path centers on preparing and explaining a recurring close; a controller/accounting manager is a plausible buyer and Revenue/AR accountants are plausible users. The external-auditor path centers on reviewing client information and documenting engagement work. The same matching engine could support both, but vocabulary, approval, evidence expectations, procurement, and success measures differ. Do not combine both into the first product claim.

Interview accountants who actually prepare Revenue/AR reconciliations, including teams using the three products above. Have them walk through their latest real workbook, identify the stubborn manual step, show the exact source exports, and explain who reviews the result. Ask whether existing licenses can already solve it, why they have not configured that capability, and who could authorize a pilot. Test the same bounded prototype with an external auditor for comparison. Select the persona with accessible users and a verified repeated problem; this evidence alone does not establish which market is commercially superior.
