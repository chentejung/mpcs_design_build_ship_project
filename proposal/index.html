Audit Workbench

Software that automates routine audit reconciliations and flags what auditors should look into.

Auditors spend days hand-building reconciliations from client journals before they can start testing. Audit Workbench turns raw journals into reconciled workpapers and a flagged list of what to look into.

The problem

During annual audit seasons, auditors often work long hours reconciling financial data, reviewing financial statements, and conducting extensive manual inspections for both public and private companies. These processes involve maintaining and updating countless Excel spreadsheets on a quarterly and annual basis, creating repetitive, time-consuming workloads.

My goal is to streamline and automate these labor-intensive workflows by developing software that handles routine reconciliations, validates financial data, and automatically detects anomalies or discrepancies. Rather than spending countless hours on repetitive tasks, auditors can focus their expertise on investigating flagged irregularities, exercising professional judgment, and performing critical audit procedures that genuinely require human oversight.

The ambitious version

Upload a client's journals and supporting schedules. The tool maps accounts to line items, rebuilds every statement and reconciliation, compares them against the statements the client prepared, and flags anything that doesn't tie or doesn't make sense across every line item. It hands back a full Excel workpaper set and a short summary of where to look deeper, with each flag linked to the relevant audit procedure and accounting standard.

How v1 works: Revenue and receivables

  1. Upload the journal entries, five supporting schedules in fixed templates, last year's closing trial balance, and the client's own trial balance.
  2. Map accounts. The AI suggests which client accounts belong to Revenue and AR, and the auditor confirms.
  3. Rebuild the lead schedule and the AR reconciliation:
    Opening AR + Credit sales ← sales register − Cash receipts ← cash receipts register − Credit notes & returns ← credit note register − Write-offs ← write-off listing = Closing AR ← aged AR listing
  4. Compare the rebuilt figures against the client's trial balance and each schedule, and flag every difference.
  5. Flag the unusual:
    • big month-over-month and year-over-year swings;
    • suspicious entries: revenue booked against accounts other than receivables or cash, manual entries in the last days of the year, round amounts, weekend postings;
    • movements that don't make sense: returns rate, days sales outstanding, sales just before year-end vs. shipping dates, cash collected after year-end.
  6. Download an Excel workpaper and read a short summary. The AI only describes flags the rules found; it never calculates numbers.

Each flag links to the procedures to perform next, from a library I write myself. It cites IFRS and ISA paragraphs and paraphrases them; their text isn't reproduced.

Testing without confidential data

Firms can't share client data with a student project, so I generate synthetic companies. A double-entry engine posts balanced transactions, so every schedule ties out. Then it plants known misstatements and records them in an answer key. This lets me measure exactly what the tool catches and what it misses.

Focus: Holistic Journey + AI Holistic AI

I want the full path to work end to end for an auditor: sign up, upload, review, download. Along the way, AI gives suggestions for the initial financial analysis, so the auditor starts from a list of what to inspect instead of a blank spreadsheet. Synthetic companies give me a correct answer to check those suggestions against:

  • Account mapping: how often does the AI map accounts correctly?
  • Detection: of the misstatements I planted, how many get flagged, and how many false flags appear?
  • Summary faithfulness: does every claim trace back to a real flag, and every citation to my procedure library?

How I'll know it works

  • Eval scores on mapping, detection, and false flags across a set of synthetic companies.
  • Three auditors work through a synthetic case and tell me which flags they would actually follow up on, and how long the same work takes by hand.

Tech stack

  • Next.js on Vercel: one deploy for the interface and the server code.
  • SheetJS: reads Excel and CSV files in the browser, so client files never reach a server. It also writes the Excel output.
  • Supabase: sign-up, synthetic companies, the procedure library, and saved mappings. No client data.
  • Gemini (free tier): mapping suggestions and summaries. It only ever sees synthetic data, because free-tier inputs may be used by the provider.
  • MCP and CLIs: Supabase MCP for the database, Vercel CLI for deploys, GitHub CLI.
Every service stays on its free tier with usage limits set. Proposal for Design, Build, Ship, Autumn 2026.
